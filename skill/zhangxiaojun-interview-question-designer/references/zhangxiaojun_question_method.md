# Zhang Xiaojun-Style Interview Question Method

Use this reference when turning a guest profile into interview questions.

## Core Interview Shape

张小珺式科技访谈通常不是“按履历提问”，而是围绕一个正在发生的技术/商业变化搭建长线追问。好的问题要让嘉宾解释：

- 他/她在什么变化里做判断；
- 为什么这个变化现在发生；
- 旧范式和新范式的差异在哪里；
- 哪些经验失效，哪些新能力变重要；
- 组织、产品、技术路线、市场结构如何随之改变。

## Question Anatomy

Prefer this structure for key questions:

```text
我看到/你过去提到/行业里现在有一个现象：……
但这里面有一个矛盾/不清楚的地方：……
所以我想问的是：……
如果拆到底层机制，它到底是怎么发生的？
```

The question can be shorter on air, but preparation notes should preserve the reasoning.

## Useful Question Types

### 1. Timeline And Inflection

- 你第一次意识到这件事发生变化，是在哪个具体时刻？
- 如果把过去几年切成几个阶段，分界点分别是什么？
- 现在这个变化和上一轮热潮最不一样的地方是什么？
- 有哪个信号让你觉得“不能再按原来的方式做了”？

### 2. Mechanism, Not Opinion

- 这个结果背后的真正机制是什么？
- 如果拆到技术/产品/组织的底层，它是哪一层先变了？
- 这里面最容易被外界误解的因果关系是什么？
- 哪个变量变化之后，整个系统的行为都变了？

### 3. Tradeoff And Allocation

- 你们当时是在什么约束下做这个选择的？
- 如果资源只能投到三件事，优先级怎么排？
- 这件事最贵的成本是什么：钱、时间、算力、人才、组织注意力，还是试错窗口？
- 有没有一个看起来正确但你后来放弃的路线？

### 4. Concrete Episode

- 能不能讲一个具体决策现场？
- 当时桌上有哪些选项？谁反对？最后怎么拍板？
- 哪个指标、用户反馈、实验结果改变了你的判断？
- 有没有一个失败样本比成功样本更说明问题？

### 5. Before And After

- 这件事发生前，你们的工作流是什么样；之后变成什么样？
- 它改变的是效率、能力边界，还是人的角色？
- 过去有效的经验，哪一部分现在反而会拖后腿？
- 新人和有经验的人，在这个新环境里谁更占优势，为什么？

### 6. Product And User Reality

- 用户真正付费/使用的是哪个价值，而不是哪个概念？
- 你们怎么判断这是刚需，不只是尝鲜？
- 产品里哪个环节最能暴露模型/技术的真实能力？
- 如果用户不用术语描述需求，他/她会怎么说这件事？

### 7. Organization And Talent

- 这个变化对团队结构提出了什么新要求？
- 什么样的人在这个阶段突然变得更有价值？
- 你作为负责人，最需要亲自盯的是什么？
- 组织里最难被改掉的旧习惯是什么？

### 8. Hard But Respectful

- 外界对你/公司/产品最大的质疑是什么？你觉得哪一条是有道理的？
- 如果这个判断错了，最可能错在哪里？
- 你们有没有把一个技术能力误判成了产品能力？
- 哪些结果还没有被证明，所以现在不能讲得太满？

### 9. Future Judgment

- 未来 12-24 个月，你最看重哪三个信号？
- 哪个共识你认为会被证明是错的？
- 如果把行业分成会受益和会受伤的两类，分界线是什么？
- 到什么时候，我们才能说这个范式真的成立了？

## Arc Patterns

### Founder / Builder

1. Live change or contradiction.
2. Personal inflection point.
3. Product or technical mechanism.
4. Decision tradeoffs and mistakes.
5. Team and execution.
6. Market structure.
7. Future judgment.

### AI Researcher / Technical Leader

1. Recent capability jump.
2. Why now: data, model, post-training, inference, tooling, or compute.
3. Research taste and failed routes.
4. Productization gap.
5. Organization and talent.
6. Open questions and limits.
7. Next paradigm.

### Top-Lab Research Scientist With A Strong Personal Voice

1. Establish the person's unusual path and temperament.
2. Ask what they saw inside frontier labs that outsiders misunderstand.
3. Separate personal contribution from collective system.
4. Probe technical prediction, research method, and confidentiality boundary.
5. Let provocative views surface, then ask for the self-consistent framework behind them.
6. Close with career advice and what kind of people the next phase needs.

### Returning Technical Founder

1. Revisit last episode's claims and ask what has been proven or falsified.
2. Explain the new product/model/company milestone.
3. Ask what changed in the founder's own understanding.
4. Test technical strategy against market validation.
5. Probe open/closed, product/platform, data flywheel, pricing, and distribution.
6. Ask which belief remains non-consensus and why they still hold it.

### CEO Operator / Product Strategist

1. Start from the CEO's current operating model.
2. Convert technology strategy into product scenes and resource allocation.
3. Ask how user life, trust, family, safety, or work actually changes.
4. Probe organizational learning and where strategy gets distorted in execution.
5. Ask how the CEO updates their own beliefs under new competition.
6. End with personal system and long-range human judgment.

### Robotics / Embodied AI Scientist

1. Distinguish software intelligence from physical-world intelligence.
2. Ask what changed after LLMs and what remains robotics-native.
3. Probe data, simulation, transfer, hardware, safety, and evaluation.
4. Move from demo to deployment.
5. Compare China/US ecosystem strengths if relevant.
6. Close with five- to ten-year scenarios.

### Oral-History Founder

1. Treat the interview as a historical record.
2. Build chronology around judgment upgrades, not resume events.
3. Use career switches as windows into taste formation.
4. Ask for decision stories where industry consensus was wrong.
5. Connect mission, company moat, and physical-world consequences.
6. Close with what the next generation should inherit or reject.

### Investor / Industry Observer

1. What changed in the market.
2. Which old framework broke.
3. How to distinguish signal from noise.
4. Case studies and counterexamples.
5. Capital allocation and timing.
6. Risks, bubbles, and second-order effects.
7. Non-consensus predictions.

### Public-Market / Silicon Valley Investor

1. Start from a market anomaly or metaphor.
2. Translate narratives into unit economics, pricing power, and capital expenditure.
3. Compare public-market and private-market incentives.
4. Ask how AI rewires software, labor, and organization.
5. Compare China/US only through concrete market structures.
6. Close with how the investor's own process changes.

### Serial Realist VC / Contrarian Investor

1. Reopen the prior episode's strongest claim.
2. Ask what changed their mind and what did not.
3. Put the bubble/consensus fight early.
4. Demand concrete portfolio or market evidence.
5. Probe platform/app/ecosystem boundaries.
6. Close with falsifiable predictions and founder advice.

## Bad Question Smells

Avoid:

- “请介绍一下你自己” as the real opening, unless the audience has no context.
- “你怎么看 AI/未来/创业” without a sharper frame.
- Questions that secretly contain the host's full answer.
- Ten questions that all ask “影响是什么”.
- Only asking success stories.
- Asking technical jargon without connecting it to product, user, or strategic consequence.

## Polish Heuristics

- For every generic question, add a named object: product, model, paper, launch, user segment, competitor, metric, or decision.
- For every opinion question, ask for evidence or a concrete episode.
- For every future question, define a time horizon and observable signal.
- For every hard question, phrase it as a serious uncertainty, not a gotcha.
